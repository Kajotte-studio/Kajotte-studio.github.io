Visual Space

Golden Rule: Light does not illuminate objects, it creates them.

In terms of physics, light is an electromagnetic wave. The phenomena recorded by the human eye and camera sensors represent only a fraction of a vast spectrum. Below is a complete, scientific categorization of electromagnetic waves, defined by strict physical parameters.

The Full Electromagnetic Spectrum

Radiation Type Wavelength (λ) Frequency (f) Phenomena / Sources
Radio Waves (ELF / AM / FM)> 1 m< 300 MHzAtmospheric phenomena, pulsars, telecommunications
Microwaves1 mm – 1 m300 MHz – 300 GHzCosmic microwave background, radar
Infrared (IR)700 nm – 1 mm300 GHz – 430 THzThermal emission, star formation regions
Visible Light (VIS)380 nm – 700 nm430 THz – 790 THzSun, lasers – optically recorded range
Ultraviolet (UV)10 nm – 380 nm790 THz – 30 PHzSolar corona, young stars (O and B types)
X-Rays0.01 nm – 10 nm30 PHz – 30 EHzBlack holes, supernova remnants

Photographer's Zone: Visible Light Structure

Camera optics and the human eye operate within a strictly defined wavelength range. It is these properties that directly define the composition, contrast, and plasticity of the image recorded in the frame.

Light Color Wavelength Optical Characteristics
Red620 nm – 700 nmLongest visible wave, strongly penetrates the atmosphere (golden hour).
Orange590 nm – 620 nmDominant wavelengths during sunrises and sunsets.
Yellow570 nm – 590 nmPeak sensitivity of the human eye in daylight.
Green495 nm – 570 nmDominant in nature; the Bayer filter allocates 50% of detectors to this range.
Blue450 nm – 495 nmStrongly scattered in the atmosphere (Rayleigh scattering).
Violet380 nm – 450 nmShortest visible wave, carrying the highest optical energy.
